Project Overview

For my final project, I plan to use two CDC datasets: waterborne and cdcmurd. I want to explore patterns in public health data across different regions and time in the United States. I will use R to clean the data, calculate summary statistics, and create plots. I also plan to write some R functions to make the analysis easier to repeat.

CDC Waterborne Illness Outbreaks

Research Question: How do seasonal patterns in reported waterborne illness outbreaks differ across regions in the United States?

Population and Unit of Analysis: The dataset contains reported outbreaks in the United States from 1971 to 2023. I will focus on records where primary_mode == “Water”. There are 3,106 records in this group. Each record represents a reported outbreak. For regional comparisons, I will use 3,079 records that can be matched to the 50 U.S. states. I will group the records by season, region, and year to calculate outbreak counts.

Exposure: The main exposures are season and geographic region. I will use the month variable to create four seasons: winter (December–February), spring (March–May), summer (June–August), and fall (September–November). I will use state.region in R to group states into four geographic regions.

Outcome: Number of reported waterborne illness outbreaks, which is a count variable.

Potential confounders: Calendar year may affect the results because reporting practices and surveillance may change over time. I will account for year by comparing seasonal and regional patterns within different time periods.

Missing data: I will check for missing values in state, month, and year. Among the 3,106 waterborne outbreak records, 27 cannot be matched to the four geographic regions because they refer to locations such as Puerto Rico, Washington, D.C., other territories, or multistate outbreaks. I will exclude these 27 records from regional comparisons and report this exclusion in the final analysis. I will also exclude records with missing month or year information when needed.

Analysis: I plan to first convert the original list into a tidy data frame. Then I will calculate outbreak counts by season and region and create grouped bar plots to compare seasonal patterns across regions. I will also write an R function to summarize outbreak counts for different groups.

CDC Homicide Mortality

Research Question: Have differences in homicide mortality rates between U.S. states increased or decreased from 2014 to 2024?

Study Population Selection: I will exclude Washington, D.C. because my research question focuses on differences between the 50 U.S. states. Washington, D.C. is a federal district rather than a state, and it differs from most states in geographic size, population size, and urbanization. These differences may affect its homicide mortality rate and make it less comparable with the states. Since my outcome is the standard deviation of state homicide mortality rates, including D.C. could also affect the results. Therefore, I will exclude D.C. based on my study population definition rather than its observed mortality rate.

Population and Unit of Analysis: I will use homicide mortality data for 50 U.S. states from 2014 to 2024. Each original record represents one state in one year. I will calculate summary statistics across states for each year, so the main trend analysis will use yearly summaries.

Exposure: Calendar year, treated as a numeric variable. I will focus on 2014–2024 and exclude records outside this period. I will check for missing or invalid year values before the analysis.

Outcome: The standard deviation (SD) of state homicide mortality rates for each year, which is a continuous summary measure. I will also calculate the interquartile range (IQR) as an additional measure of differences between states.

Potential confounders: Differences in age, sex and firearm ownership distributions may affect homicide mortality rates across states. However, these variables are not available in this dataset, so I will not be able to adjust for them. This analysis will focus on describing changes in state-level variation over time rather than estimating a causal effect of calendar year. I will discuss these factors as possible limitations in the final analysis.

Missing Data: From 2014 to 2024, there are 550 state-year records for the 50 U.S. states, excluding Washington, D.C. Among these records, 25 have missing homicide mortality rates, leaving 525 available records. For the main analysis, I will calculate SD and IQR using states with available death rates in each year, without imputing missing values. I will report the number of available states each year. As a sensitivity analysis, I will repeat the calculations using only states with complete data for all 11 years to check whether missing data affect the observed trends.

Analysis: I will calculate SD and IQR of state homicide mortality rates for each year and I will create line plots to show whether differences between states have increased or decreased over time. I also plan to write an R function to calculate these statistics for different years.
